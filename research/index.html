Ticket-routing preprint examines when AI should hand decisions to people

The authors compare trained classifiers and prompted language models under explicit costs for correct routing, human triage and mistakes. Their abstract reports that abstention policies can reduce routing cost, while better calibration alone need not improve a model's ability to distinguish correct answers. The findings are limited to the tested dataset, prompts and illustrative costs.

MIT and Harvard researchers introduce Role Anchor to reduce role drift in compound AI systems

Researchers at MIT and Harvard describe a failure mode they call role drift, in which modules inside compound AI systems deviate from their intended functions while overall end-to-end accuracy still improves. They introduce a method called Role Anchor that aims to keep modules constrained to their assigned roles during training, including in retrieval-augmented generation pipelines where a reader might otherwise answer from internal memory instead of retrieved evidence.

Stanford researchers describe a 37,000-agent 'virtual biotech' system for AI-driven drug discovery

At VentureBeat's Transform 2026 event, Stanford professor James Zou described a multi-agent research system that scales from small 'virtual lab' teams to a 37,000-agent 'virtual biotech' organized into divisions such as target discovery, molecule design, and clinical trials. Zou said the system uses an AI-native data layer called Paperclip to let agents work across scientific literature and databases more effectively, and reported internal results including nanobody design and an antibody-drug conjugate concept targeting CD276 for lung cancer. He also said Merck later independently developed and validated the same therapeutic design, which he presented as external confirmation of the system's output.
